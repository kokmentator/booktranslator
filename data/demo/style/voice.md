# The author — Lewis Carroll (demo notes)

Victorian mathematician writing for children without ever writing down to them.
The narrator is deadpan: the more absurd the event, the calmer the telling.
Logic-play is the engine of the humour — puns, syllogisms taken literally,
polite arguments about nonsense. Keep Alice's interior voice prim, earnest and
slightly bossy; she reasons her way through impossible things.

Must survive translation: the parenthetical asides ("which was very likely true"),
the capitalised nouns for Important Things, and wordplay — recreate a pun in the
target language rather than translating it literally and killing it.
